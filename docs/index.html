Build guide

Flip-down mount for the kitchen tablet

A tablet hangs from a friction hinge under the wall cabinet. Flip it down to cook, and afterwards it disappears flat under the cabinet floor. No holes in the tablet: a small aluminium plate carries the load.

Tablet

Ganter GN 437, 40 mm, adjustable friction

How it works

  • A 2 mm aluminium plate is glued to the back of the tablet. The hinge is screwed to this plate, not to the tablet. So the force from flipping spreads over almost the whole back.
  • The second leaf of the hinge is screwed to the cabinet floor. The friction in the hinge holds the tablet in any position.
  • Flipped up, a pot magnet in the cabinet floor holds a steel disc on the plate. The tablet then lies flat, display facing down.
  • A flat cable and a USB-C coupler stay fixed on the tablet. Only the charging cable moves, in a short loop over the axis.
  • A custom app uses the accelerometer to tell whether the tablet is up or down, and turns the display off and on. Details below.

Build

Before you start: Screw plate and hinge together first, then glue them to the tablet. Nothing is drilled on the tablet itself. The VHB tape needs a day before it carries full load.

  1. Mark the plate

    Lay the hinge flush with one long edge of the plate, centred. Mark the hole centres through the two holes of the leaf. This edge later goes to the long tablet edge the tablet pivots on.

  2. Drill and countersink

    Drill through the plate with 3.2 mm. Then countersink from the side that goes onto the tablet, until the screw head sits flush.

    Check: Run a fingernail over the countersink. Nothing may catch, or the head presses on the tablet.

  3. Screw the hinge to the plate

    Push the M3 countersunk screws from the tablet side through plate and leaf, washer and nut on top. Tighten firmly: the M3 has play in the leaf's hole, only the clamping holds it.

    Why M3: The sheet must be at least as thick as the bolt head is high. An M3 countersunk head (1.7 mm) fits in 2 mm; M5 (2.8 mm) would need 3 mm. Otherwise the countersink cuts through and leaves only a knife edge. The load is small: about 0.22 N·m to hold, at most about 2 N·m of friction on two bolts.

  4. Glue the plate to the tablet

    Degrease the tablet back and the plate with isopropyl alcohol. Lay VHB strips side by side on the plate, almost full surface. Set the plate flush with the pivot edge and centred on the long side, under the hinge. Press hard.

    Wait: per 3M, 50 % bond strength after 20 minutes, 90 % after 24 hours. Mount it the next day.

  5. Stick on the steel disc

    Stick the self-adhesive disc from the magnet set onto the plate, near the corner opposite the hinge. There the magnet has the longest lever.

  6. Cable on the tablet

    Plug the 75 mm flat cable into the USB-C port and fold it flat towards the back. Glue the coupler with VHB in the strip next to the plate and plug in the flat cable. The charging cable goes into the coupler. So no pull acts on the tablet's port.

  7. Screw the hinge to the cabinet

    Hold the tablet flipped up under the cabinet and align it. Hold the free leaf against the cabinet floor near the front edge, drill pilot holes and fix it with two wood screws.

    Note: Screwing into the cabinet is not recommended. Glue hinge and magnets to the cabinet floor instead. I screwed them anyway.

  8. Fit the magnet

    Stack two pot magnets and screw them into the cabinet floor with one wood screw, exactly above the steel disc. The stack makes up for the height of the hinge, so the tablet lies straight when flipped up.

    Check: Flip it up. The disc snaps to the magnet and the tablet stays up.

  9. Route the charging cable

    Lead the charging cable in a short loop over the axis, then along the underside of the cabinet to the wall. Cover it with furniture film in the cabinet's finish, so it is hard to see. The USB-A power supply goes at the end.

    Check: Flipped up, the cable lies next to the tablet, not under it.

  10. Set the friction

    Loosen the adjusting screw on the hinge (2.5 mm hex) a lot, then tighten it in small steps until the tablet holds every position. At most 0.5 N·m.

    Check: The tablet stays where you put it and moves with light pressure. If it sinks: a bit tighter. If it is stiff: loosen a bit.

The app: display off when flipped up

The tablet runs as a kiosk with a custom Android app. It is the home screen and shows the recipes from a self-hosted Mealie instance. The code is not published, so here is how it works, for your own build.

Detect the position. A foreground service reads the accelerometer, also with the display off. A partial wake lock keeps the CPU awake for this. From the gravity vector it computes the angle between display and floor: 0° means flat with the display facing down, 90° means vertical.

Switch with hysteresis. From 50° the tablet counts as flipped down, below 40° as flipped up. The angle must be stable for 400 ms. So nothing switches when you tap the screen or while it swings.

Turn off. Flipped up, the app calls DevicePolicyManager.lockNow(). For this it is a device administrator, but only with the force-lock policy.

Turn on. Flipped down, a short wake lock (FULL_WAKE_LOCK with ACQUIRE_CAUSES_WAKEUP, 3 s) brings the display back. Then the app restarts its activity. The activity may sit above the lock screen (setShowWhenLocked) and keeps the display on while it is visible.

Trap: The activity must not also set setTurnScreenOn(true). Every restart of the activity re-arms this flag. On the next lockNow() the lock screen appears, the activity becomes visible above it and turns the display back on 14 ms later. Measured on this tablet.

Setup via adb

adb shell cmd role add-role-holder android.app.role.HOME <package> 0
adb shell dpm set-active-admin <package>/.LockAdmin
adb shell settings put global stay_on_while_plugged_in 0
adb shell dumpsys deviceidle whitelist +<package>
adb shell pm grant <package> android.permission.POST_NOTIFICATIONS
adb shell appops set <package> SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW allow
Home screen
The app starts by itself after every reboot and brings the sensor service with it.
Device admin
Needed for lockNow(). Without it the display simply stays on.
stay_on_while_plugged_in
Must be 0. Otherwise the permanently connected charging cable keeps the display on.
Battery exemption
So Android does not put the service to sleep.
Notifications
Samsung cancels the wake-up if the app may not post notifications.
Overlay permission
Without it Android blocks the activity start from the service. The display then lights for 3 s and goes off again.

Permanent power is not a problem, because Samsung's battery protection stops charging at 80 %.

Technical appendix

  1. Hinge GN 437, 40 mm, centred on the pivot edge. Hole spacing 25 mm.
  2. Aluminium plate 100 × 150 × 2 mm, flush with the pivot edge, centred.
  3. Steel disc for the magnet stack.
  4. Flat cable 75 mm from the USB-C port.
  5. USB-C coupler, glued with VHB.
  6. Charging cable 2 m, loop over the axis.
  7. Camera stays free.
Holding torque
about 0.22 N·m (366 g, centre of mass about 62 mm from the axis), calculated
Hinge friction
up to about 2 N·m, so plenty of reserve. That is why it needs fine adjustment.
Why flush
The hinge holes sit 12.5 mm from the axis. Flush with the edge they sit about 11 mm deep in the plate, enough material around the 3.2 mm holes.
Why M3
An M3 countersunk head is 1.7 mm high and fits in 2 mm sheet. M5 would need 3 mm, otherwise only a knife edge is left.
Why a plate
A leaf glued straight to the tablet would be peeled off at its front edge with every flip. The plate spreads the load over almost the whole back.
